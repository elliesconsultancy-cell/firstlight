Learning to code is a bit like learning to swim: reading about it helps, but you only really learn by getting in the water. This lesson explains how Firstlight is organised, so you always know what to do next.

## The big picture

Firstlight is split into **weeks**. Each week has a topic, a few **lessons**, and an **assignment**. You can study at your own pace, but we recommend keeping up with one week at a time. Small, steady steps beat one giant weekend of studying.

Here is what a week looks like:

1. **Read the week overview.** It tells you what the week is about and what you will be able to do at the end.
2. **Work through the lessons.** Read them, run the examples, and do every "Try it" exercise.
3. **Mark each lesson as done.** This helps you (and your instructor) see your progress.
4. **Do the assignment.** This is where you build something real with what you learned.
5. **Submit your assignment.** You can send a written answer, a link, a file, or a mix.
6. **Get feedback.** Your instructor reads your work and replies. They may approve it, or ask you to change a few things.
7. **Make changes if needed, then move on.** Feedback is not a bad mark. It is a gift.

> 💡 **Tip:** Pick fixed times in your week for studying, like "Tuesday and Thursday evenings, 7pm to 9pm". It is much easier to keep a habit when it has a time and a place.

## Lessons: read, then do

Every lesson has small pieces of code. Do not just read them. Type them out yourself, run them, and change things to see what happens. Typing code (instead of copying it) helps your brain and your fingers remember.

When you see a code block like this one, you can press **Try in playground** to run it:

```html
<h1>Hello, Firstlight!</h1>
<p>This is my first time running code.</p>
```

Each lesson ends with **Check your understanding** questions. Try to answer them in your head (or on paper) before you open the answers. Getting an answer wrong is fine. It shows you what to read again.

## Assignments and feedback

The assignment is the most important part of each week. It is where you find out what you really understood.

When you submit, your instructor will look at it and give you feedback. There are two common results:

- **Approved** – great work, you can continue.
- **Changes requested** – your instructor has suggestions. Read them carefully, update your work, and submit again.

Professional developers get feedback on their code every single day. It is called **code review**, and even very senior developers get comments like "please rename this" or "this could be simpler". Getting used to feedback now is real job practice.

> 🧠 **Remember:** "Changes requested" does not mean "you failed". It means "you are close, here is how to get closer".

## Being a good learner

A few habits make a huge difference:

- **Little and often.** Thirty minutes on four days is better than two hours on one day.
- **Take notes in your own words.** If you can explain an idea simply, you understand it.
- **Ask questions early.** If you are stuck for more than 30 minutes, ask for help. (We will practise how in a later lesson.)
- **Compare yourself only to yesterday's you.** Other learners have different backgrounds and different amounts of time. Your progress is your progress.
- **Rest.** Your brain keeps working on problems while you sleep. Many people find the answer the next morning.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** It is very normal to feel confused or slow in the first weeks. Almost everyone feels this. It is not a sign that coding is "not for you". It is a sign that you are learning something new.

## How much time do you need?

Most weeks need about **5 to 8 hours**. Some people need more, some need less. That is fine. If life gets busy (work, family, illness), tell your instructor. A short message like "I am behind this week because of work, I will catch up by Sunday" is always welcome.
