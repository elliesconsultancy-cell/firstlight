You have probably heard a lot about AI. Some people say it will do everything. Others say it is only hype. This week we ignore both and look at what is really going on, in plain words and with pictures.

First, you will learn what **AI**, **machine learning** and a **language model** are, and how they fit together. Then we will open the box and see how a language model makes its sentences. The big idea is simple: it is a very well-read autocomplete.

Then we will look at what AI does well, where it fails, and why it sometimes makes things up. You will meet **Amina**, who runs a small bakery and wants an AI helper. Over this course, you will help her build it. It is called **BakeBuddy**.

Finally, you will interview a real AI yourself. You will run five small tests and write down what you find. No code is needed this week.

## By the end of this week you will be able to

- Explain AI, machine learning and a language model in your own words
- Describe how a language model writes text, one word at a time
- Say clearly what a language model is not: not a person, not a search engine
- Name three things AI does well and three things it does badly
- Spot and check an answer that may be made up (a hallucination)

## Day plan

### Learning objectives
- Explain AI, machine learning and a language model with the three boxes picture.
- Explain a language model as a very well-read autocomplete that guesses the next word.
- Say what a language model is not: a person, or a search engine.
- Describe a hallucination (a confident guesser) and name two ways to check an answer.

### Purpose
This is the first sprint, so it sets the pictures the whole course uses: the autocomplete, the confident guesser, and the running project BakeBuddy. Later weeks (tokens, prompts, API) all build on these ideas. It also sets a healthy habit: use AI, and check it.

### Agenda
1. **What is AI?** Teach the three boxes and the two "not" pictures (not a person, not a search engine). Learners do the Try it: find three machine learning things on their phone. Introduce Amina and BakeBuddy.
2. **How a language model works.** Teach the autocomplete and the next-word chances with the baker sentence. Learners play the next-word guesser. Mention training and that its knowledge has a date.
3. **What AI is good and bad at.** Teach the strengths and weak spots, then hallucination and the checking habits. Learners run the invented book test.
4. **Interview an AI.** Launch it with a live demo of one test, such as counting the letter e. Walk around and look at their test choices and notes.
