A language model does not read the way you do. It does not see letters or whole words. It sees small chunks of text, and it can only look at a limited amount at one time. Knowing this will save you from many confusing problems later.

This week has three pictures. **Tokens** are Lego bricks of text. The **context window** is the size of the desk the model works on. **Temperature** is how adventurous the model is when it picks the next word.

These three ideas also explain the price of AI. Companies that offer models usually charge by the amount of text you send and receive. So you will learn to estimate how much a product like BakeBuddy might cost to run.

There is a little code this week, but only tiny scripts that run in your browser. No account and no secret keys are needed.

## By the end of this week you will be able to

- Explain what a token is and estimate the tokens in a piece of text
- Explain what the context window is and what happens when it is full
- Explain what temperature does and when to choose a low or high value
- Describe why AI usage costs money and what makes a request cheap or expensive
- Estimate the size and cost of one BakeBuddy conversation

## Day plan

### Learning objectives
- Explain a token and estimate tokens in a text.
- Explain the context window and what happens when it is full.
- Explain temperature and choose low or high for a task.
- Describe what makes an AI request cheap or expensive.
- Estimate the size and cost of one BakeBuddy conversation.

### Purpose
Last week learners met the autocomplete. This week they learn its three numbers: tokens, context and temperature. These explain size limits, price and the way answers vary. They lead straight into Week 2 (prompts) and Week 3 (the first API call, where real token counts appear).

### Agenda
1. **Tokens.** Teach the Lego picture and the 4 characters rule. Learners run the estimator and try the Try it examples.
2. **The context window.** Teach the desk and what shares it. Use Tolu's long chat to show why the desk fills and why costs grow.
3. **Temperature and cost.** Teach vanilla versus adventure, then input and output pricing. Stress that prices in the lesson are made up and learners must check the pricing page.
4. **BakeBuddy numbers.** Launch with the 5-message chat sum on a board. Then look at learners' sums and labels on prices.
