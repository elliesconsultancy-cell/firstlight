## What you'll build

Your very first web page: an **About Me** page. It tells visitors who you are, what you like, and why you are learning to code.

It does not need to look fancy. We have not learned CSS yet. What matters is clean, correct HTML.

## Requirements

- [ ] The file is called `index.html` and lives in a folder called `about-me`
- [ ] It uses the full HTML boilerplate (`<!DOCTYPE html>`, `<html lang="en">`, `<head>`, `<body>`)
- [ ] The `<title>` contains your name
- [ ] There is one `<h1>` heading with your name or a greeting
- [ ] There are at least two `<p>` paragraphs about you
- [ ] There is at least one list (`<ul>` or `<ol>`) with three or more items
- [ ] There is at least one image (`<img>`) with a helpful `alt` description
- [ ] There is at least one link (`<a>`) to another website
- [ ] Every element that needs a closing tag has one
- [ ] Your code is indented, so people can read it clearly

## Steps/hints

1. In your `firstlight/week-01` folder, create a folder called `about-me`. Inside it, create `index.html` and a folder called `images`.
2. Start with the boilerplate. In VS Code, type `!` and press **Tab**.
3. Plan your content first, in plain words. For example:
   - A greeting with your name
   - Where you are from (share only what you are happy to share)
   - Why you are learning to code
   - A list of hobbies, favourite foods, or goals for this course
   - A picture (you, your town, your pet, or something you love)
   - A link to a website you find useful or interesting
4. Now add the HTML tags around your content.
5. For the image, choose one:
   - Put a photo in your `images` folder and use `src="images/your-photo.jpg"`, **or**
   - Use an online image URL, like `https://picsum.photos/400/300`
6. Save often. Refresh the browser to check your work.
7. Open DevTools (`F12` → **Elements**). Check that the structure looks as you expect.

> 💡 **Tip:** If something looks wrong, read your code out loud to your "rubber duck", tag by tag. Check that every `<p>` has a `</p>`, every `<li>` is inside a `<ul>` or `<ol>`, and your image path is spelled exactly right.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** Share only personal information you are comfortable with. You do not need your full name, your address, or a photo of yourself. A photo of something you like is fine.

## How to submit

Use the submit form on this page. You have two options:

- **Option A: Upload your file.** Upload your `index.html`. If you used a photo from your computer, upload the image file too.
- **Option B: Share a CodePen link.** Go to codepen.io and create a free account. Click **Pen**. Paste only the part **inside** your `<body>` into the HTML box. Save it and paste the link into the link box. (Online image URLs work best on CodePen.)

In the written answer box, write one or two sentences: what was the most comfortable part, and what was the hardest?

## Stretch goals

- Add a second page, `hobbies.html`, in the same folder. Link to it from `index.html`. Link back from `hobbies.html` to `index.html`. Use relative paths.
- Use both a `<ul>` and an `<ol>` on your page, each where it makes sense.
- Use `<strong>` and `<em>` in your paragraphs to mark important words.
- Add an HTML comment (`<!-- like this -->`) above each section to explain it.
