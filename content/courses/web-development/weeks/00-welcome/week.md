Welcome to Firstlight! You do not need any experience. You need curiosity, a computer, and a little time every week. Everybody here started at zero once, including your instructor.

This first week is not about writing lots of code. It is about getting ready. Think of the first day at a new job. Before you do the real work, you find your desk, learn where things are, and meet the team.

This week you will:

- learn how a normal week on the course works,
- install the tools that web developers use every day,
- practise the most important skill in programming: **knowing what to do when you are stuck**,
- learn how to use AI tools so they help you learn, and do not do the learning for you.

At the end of the week you introduce yourself. Then we can get to know you and help you in the best way.

## By the end of this week you will be able to

- Explain the weekly rhythm of the course: read, practise, submit, get feedback
- Open Chrome DevTools and VS Code, and create a folder for your course work
- Use a simple routine to get unstuck: rubber duck, read the error, search, ask
- Write a good question that includes context, what you tried, and what you expected
- Describe when AI tools help your learning and when they hurt it

## Day plan

### Learning objectives
- Explain how a normal week on the course works
- Open Chrome DevTools and VS Code, and create hello.html in the firstlight folder
- Follow the get-unstuck routine and write a four-part question
- Describe good and bad uses of AI while learning

### Purpose
This is the first week, so the aim is confidence and a working setup, not content. A learner whose tools work and who knows how to ask for help is ready for week 1 (how the web works). Their introduction also tells you who needs extra support.

### Agenda
1. **How this course works.** Cover the weekly rhythm, feedback and "changes requested". Learners say when they will study.
2. **Setting up your tools.** Everyone installs Chrome and VS Code, makes `firstlight/week-00`, and opens `hello.html` in the browser. Help anyone whose install failed.
3. **How to get unstuck and ask good questions.** Teach the four steps and the four parts of a good question. Try the rubber duck on the broken HTML example.
4. **Using AI tools as a learner.** Teach the gym picture and the delete test. Agree how learners should say when AI helped.
5. **Introduce yourself.** Launch the assignment. Show the screenshot and the submit form. Check each learner has a screenshot ready.
