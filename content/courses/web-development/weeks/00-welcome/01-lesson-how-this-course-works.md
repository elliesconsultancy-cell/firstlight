Think about learning to swim. You can read a book about swimming, but you only learn when you get in the water. Coding is the same. This lesson shows you how Firstlight works, so you always know what to do next.

## What a week looks like

Firstlight is split into **weeks**. Each week has a topic, a few **lessons** (short texts with examples), and one **assignment** (a small project you build and send to us).

You can study at your own pace. We suggest one week at a time. Small, steady steps work better than one giant weekend of study.

Here are the steps of a normal week:

1. **Read the week overview.** It tells you what you will be able to do at the end.
2. **Do the lessons.** Read them, run the examples, and do every "Try it".
3. **Mark each lesson as done.** You and your instructor can then see your progress.
4. **Do the assignment.** You build something real with what you learned.
5. **Submit it.** You can send text, a link, a file, or a mix.
6. **Get feedback.** Your instructor reads your work and replies.
7. **Make changes if needed, then move on.**

> 💡 **Tip:** Choose fixed study times, like "Tuesday and Thursday, 7pm to 9pm". A habit lasts longer when it has a time and a place.

## Lessons: read, then do

Every lesson has small pieces of code. Do not only read them. Type them yourself, run them, and change them to see what happens. When you type code, your brain and your fingers both remember it.

A code block like this one has a **Try it** button. Press it to run the code:


Each lesson ends with **Check your understanding** questions. Answer them in your head or on paper first. Then open the answers. A wrong answer is fine. It shows you what to read again.

## Assignments and feedback

The assignment is the most important part of the week. It shows you what you really understood.

When you submit, your instructor gives you feedback. There are two common results:

- **Approved:** great work, you can continue.
- **Changes requested:** your instructor has ideas to improve your work. Read them, update your work, and submit again.

Professional developers get feedback every day. It is called **code review**. Even very senior developers hear "please rename this" or "this could be shorter". So you are practising for a real job.

> 🧠 **Remember:** "Changes requested" does not mean "you failed". It means "you are close, and here is how to get closer".

## Habits that help

- **Little and often.** Thirty minutes on four days is better than two hours on one day.
- **Take notes in your own words.** If you can explain an idea in plain words, you understand it.
- **Ask early.** If you are stuck for more than 30 minutes, ask for help. A later lesson shows you how.
- **Compare yourself only to yesterday's you.** Other learners have different time and different backgrounds.
- **Rest.** Your brain keeps working while you sleep. Many people find the answer the next morning.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** In the first weeks you will feel confused or slow. Almost everyone does. It does not mean coding is "not for you". It means you are learning something new.

## How much time do you need?

Most weeks need about **5 to 8 hours**. Some people need more, some less. That is fine.

If life gets busy (work, family, illness), tell your instructor. A short message is welcome, for example: "I am behind this week because of work. I will catch up by Sunday."
